Normalised Counts data

Filter low count genes

The authors of the original paper did not filter low-count genes before normalisation (nor do they mention such action), so we used the raw counts to identify them. Genes with consistently low read counts were removed to reduce their influence on the analysis: only genes with at least 10 counts in a minimum of 80 samples were retained

clean_counts_raw_df <- counts_raw_df |>
  filter(rowSums(across(-1) >= 10) >= 80)

We use semi_join() to remove the same genes from the normalised data. This function keeps only the rows of counts_norm whose gene_id also appears in the filtered raw data, without adding any columns from it.

clean_counts_norm_df <- counts_norm_df |>
  semi_join(clean_counts_raw_df, by = "gene_id")

Descriptive Analysis of Gene Expression

We wanted to see how gene expression differed across the three experimental conditions. We used the naïve condition as our reference because those cells were neither exposed to IFNγ nor infected with Salmonella. By comparing each condition with the same reference, we could see how large the differences were and how much they varied from gene to gene.

1. Mean Expression Differences Relative to Naïve

We used the mean normalized log2 expression values already calculated in gene_summary. To see how each gene’s expression differed from the untreated condition, we matched its mean in each experimental condition with its mean in the naïve condition and subtracted the naïve mean. This gave every gene its own baseline and let us compare how much expression differed across the three conditions. Positive values mean higher expression than in naïve, negative values mean lower expression, and zero means the two means were equal.

expression_changes <- gene_summary |>
  select(gene_id, condition_name, mean_counts) |>
  # Put the four condition means side by side for each gene
  pivot_wider(
    names_from = condition_name,
    values_from = mean_counts
  ) |>
  # Create one row per gene and experimental condition
  pivot_longer(
    cols = c(IFNg, SL1344, IFNg_SL1344),
    names_to = "condition",
    values_to = "condition_mean"
  ) |>
  mutate(expression_change = condition_mean - naive)

2. Distribution of Gene Expression Differences

We drew one histogram for each experimental condition. The horizontal axis shows the difference in mean expression from naïve. Each bar counts how many genes had a difference within that range. Taller bars show where more genes are grouped, and a wider histogram shows that the differences are more spread out across genes.

# Visualisation with histograms
plot_expression <- expression_changes |>
  ggplot(aes(x = expression_change,
             fill = condition)) +

  geom_histogram(bins = 50,
                 alpha = 0.7) +

  facet_wrap(~ condition,
             nrow = 1) +

  labs(title = "Gene Expression Changes Compared with Naïve",
       x = "Difference in mean normalized log2 expression from naïve",
       y = "Number of genes",
       caption = paste0(
         "Data: Alasoo et al. (2018) | Naïve reference: authors' analysis code\n",
         "Data reshaping: tidyr | Histograms and panels: ggplot2"
       )) +

  theme_minimal() +

  theme(
    legend.position = "none",
    plot.title.position = "plot",
    plot.title = element_text(hjust = 0.5),
    plot.caption.position = "plot",
    plot.caption = element_text(hjust = 0.5)
  )

plot_expression

Results

In all three histograms, the tallest bars were close to zero. The IFNγ condition had the narrowest peak, with differences clustered close to zero. The differences were more spread out in the SL1344 condition. The combined IFNγ and SL1344 condition had the widest spread around its peak.
